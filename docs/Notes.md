# Initial Ideas

1. Project Overview and Vision
One-line positioning:
Build the world's first AI-driven autonomous electronic bill of lading (eBL) and supply chain finance agent on the Injective blockchain, automating the entire flow from document transfer to automatic financing, in compliance with international standards such as MLETR / eUCP / DCSA.
Core vision:
Upgrade Lixun Technology's existing BL digitisation system into an eBL platform that is legally transferable + financeable at a financial-grade level + driven by autonomous AI decisions, turning supply chain finance from "manual review + manual disbursement" into "AI judgement + automatic on-chain triggers".

2. Market Background and Pain Points (why this is valuable now)

On May 1, 2026, China's new Maritime Code took effect, introducing MLETR principles and giving eBLs clear legal status in China for the first time.
Global supply chains still rely heavily on paper bills of lading, with long financing cycles, high costs and high fraud risk.
Most existing eBL platforms stop at "digitised workflows" and lack legal-grade document control + financial-grade automated financing.
Injective has clear advantages in RWA and Agent infrastructure (iAgent 2.0 + Agent SDK), making it well suited to "autonomous financial agents".

Core pain points:

Trusted document transfer is hard (uniqueness, control, tamper-resistance)
Financing review is slow and labour-intensive
Cross-border compliance is complex (MLETR, eUCP, DCSA)
Lack of AI Agents that can truly "decide and execute autonomously"

## Partnership Proposal

ENI × Lixun Technology: Electronic Bill of Lading (eBL) and Supply Chain Finance Partnership Proposal

I. Partnership Background

Global supply chains are digitising rapidly, and the electronic bill of lading (eBL) has become core infrastructure for logistics, cross-border trade and supply chain finance.
Lixun Technology has deep capabilities in logistics digitisation, supply chain management and omnichannel e-commerce middle platforms;
ENI focuses on underlying technologies such as trusted document transfer, tamper-resistance, uniqueness control and cross-enterprise document exchange.

The two parties' capabilities are highly complementary, and together they can build a financial-grade eBL solution that drives real adoption of supply chain finance and cross-border trade.
